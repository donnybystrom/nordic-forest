# Nordic Forest project brief

Build a convincing, playable northern Swedish forest with a practical biome authoring workflow. A small village exists within the forest's physical and supernatural ecology: its inhabitants regard folklore beings as real neighbors, hazards or powers. The supernatural should emerge through place, sound and behavior as well as creature design.

## First experience

Proposed first slice: walk from a village boundary through a dry pine rise, a mesic spruce and mixed woodland, and a damp hollow. A short return route should show recognizable transitions, ground layers, varied tree age, deadwood and readable paths. Start with approximately 128 by 128 metres and a 150–250 metre route; adjust after terrain and performance measurements. These are production starting points, not approved world limits.

Use first-person walking as a temporary inspection camera, not a final camera decision. Defer a full settlement, combat, crafting, multiplayer, seasons and a large bestiary until forest authoring and one encounter work. Keep one seasonal condition initially. The exact Norrland locality, historical period, camera and survival loop remain product choices.

## Quality priorities

1. Forest identity: species silhouettes, understory, ground materials, believable variation and transitions.
2. Player experience: scale, contact with terrain, legible routes, sound and movement through vegetation.
3. Art direction: controlled palette and light, convincing wet/dry surfaces and atmospheric depth.
4. Authoring: a seed and editable fields generate a useful starting point; brushes, exclusion zones and pinned landmarks preserve intent.
5. Performance: repeatable measurements on the actual target Mac, with separate quality profiles for stronger machines.

Photorealistic source textures alone do not establish a convincing forest. Scale, distribution, material response, lighting and composition must be reviewed together. Select a few coherent asset families before expanding the library.

## Authority and evidence

The human owns product priorities, visual identity and acceptance of representative scene images. Technical Lead owns technical choices within that scope. QA checks independent criteria and actual artifacts; a second agent agreeing is not verification. Planning outputs cannot advance game features to verified.

Keep ecological observations, inferred relationships, art-directed placement rules, regional folklore and original fiction separately labeled. Each accepted feature needs its editable source, reproduction steps, measured or visual evidence and known limitations. Manual paint/erase and engine import must be exercised in the actual tools before the authoring workflow is accepted.
